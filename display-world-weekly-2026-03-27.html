LCD / Oxide TFT

LG Display's Oxide 1Hz: How Low-Leakage TFTs Bring LTPO-Like Efficiency to LCD

LG Display / NasdaqMar 21, 2026

LG Display's Oxide 1Hz technology achieves variable refresh rate down to 1Hz on an LCD panel by combining low-power-leakage oxide thin-film transistors with proprietary circuit algorithms that maintain image stability at ultra-low refresh rates. Previously, this capability was exclusive to LTPO OLED panels in premium smartphones. By bringing it to LCD, LG enables laptop manufacturers to offer LTPO-like battery efficiency at a lower cost and with the brightness advantages of LCD.

  • Low-leakage oxide TFTs maintain pixel charge at 1Hz without image degradation
  • Circuit algorithms compensate for TFT variability at ultra-low refresh rates
  • First time LTPO-like VRR efficiency achieved on a laptop-class LCD panel
  • Roadmap: 1Hz capability coming to OLED laptop panels in 2027

Hybrid Display

Monolithic Micro-LED + OLED Integration Achieves 90.89% sRGB — No Mass Transfer Required

Displays JournalMar 24, 2026

Researchers published a breakthrough hybrid display architecture in the journal Displays, integrating GaN-based blue Micro-LED arrays with red and green OLEDs on a single substrate — eliminating the need for mass transfer of all three colour channels. Through optimised KOH wet etching and ITO thickness tuning, the team achieved a 92.5% increase in blue Micro-LED luminance and 90.89% sRGB colour gamut coverage at 4.5V drive voltage.

  • Blue Micro-LED + red/green OLED on single substrate — no mass transfer needed
  • 92.5% increase in blue Micro-LED luminance via KOH treatment optimisation
  • 90.89% sRGB colour gamut at 4.5V — competitive with standalone OLED
  • Blue Micro-LED: 4.86 × 10⁴ cd/m² at 4.5V; green OLED: 87 cd/m² at 5V

Flexible OLED

Stretchable OLEDs Match Rigid Device Performance — Nature Materials Breakthrough

Nature Materials / Argonne National LabMar 2026

A research team published a landmark study in Nature Materials demonstrating stretchable OLEDs that match the performance of rigid devices — a first in the field. By developing new stretchable electron transport layers (ETL) using a novel ETL polymer and liquid-metal AlGaIn cathode layers, the researchers enabled efficient electron injection even under significant mechanical deformation, opening the door to high-efficiency wearable and medical display applications.

  • First stretchable OLED to match rigid device efficiency metrics
  • Novel ETL polymer + liquid-metal AlGaIn cathode enables electron injection under strain
  • Published in Nature Materials — highest-impact materials science journal
  • Enables high-efficiency wearable, medical, and skin-conformable display applications
